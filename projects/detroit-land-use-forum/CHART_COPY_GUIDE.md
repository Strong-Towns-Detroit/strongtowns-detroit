# Chart copy guide

This guide governs audience-facing text in Detroit Land Use Forum charts and
maps. Methodology documents can be exhaustive; exhibit copy should make the
evidence understandable at a glance.

## Editorial hierarchy

1. **Headline — the finding.** State the most important supported pattern in
   active, concrete language. A topic label is not a headline.
2. **Subtitle — the comparison.** Define what is measured, the relevant
   population, geography, period, or legal threshold.
3. **Labels — how to read the marks.** Prefer plain, direct labels close to the
   data. Use legal terminology only where the legal distinction matters.
4. **Annotation — why a feature matters.** Annotate only consequential
   patterns, exceptions, or reference points.
5. **Footer — scope and verification.** State the unit, denominator, dates,
   sources, material assumptions, and limitations that could change the
   audience's interpretation.
6. **Accessible description — the visual evidence in words.** Summarize the
   chart form, principal pattern, important values, and exclusions without
   repeating the headline verbatim.

## Evaluation framework

Score each public-facing line against these questions:

- **Audience:** Does it speak directly to a forum visitor rather than narrate
  the analyst's workflow?
- **Finding:** Does the headline say what the evidence shows?
- **Support:** Can the visible data sustain every word of the claim?
- **Specificity:** Are the denominator, time period, geography, units, and
  legal boundary available where needed?
- **Plain language:** Can a non-specialist understand it without losing the
  underlying legal meaning?
- **Economy:** Does each line do one job, without repeating another line?
- **Placement:** Is interpretive text prominent and methodological text
  subordinate?
- **Neutrality:** Does wording distinguish a measured fact, a legal
  consequence, and an analytical estimate?
- **Accessibility:** Would the title and description orient a reader who
  cannot inspect the marks?

## House rules

- Use a descriptive title such as “Detroit's 5,000-square-foot minimum lot
  area” when a map primarily applies a legal threshold. Reserve finding-style
  titles for conclusions that add meaning beyond restating the dominant map
  color.
- Prefer counts and ordinary nouns to pipeline terms such as “corpus,”
  “classification,” “candidate,” “matched,” or “false positive.”
- Do not tell the audience what the chart “does not mean” unless a foreseeable
  misreading would materially change the conclusion. State the applicable fact
  instead.
- Do not display implementation decisions such as clipping, deduplication
  repairs, manual review, or model cleanup. Put them in methodology.
- Keep consequential scope limits visible: sampled cases, unmapped records,
  measurement tolerance, modeled rather than observed quantities, and
  counterfactual assumptions.
- Use “case” for a deduplicated BZA matter and define repeats or multiple relief
  assignments in the footer when relevant.
- Titles use sentence case and avoid questions unless the graphic immediately
  answers them.
- Legends name what the color means, not the internal field value.
